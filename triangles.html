Triangles

At its core, a triangle (sometimes called a trigon) is one of the simplest and most fundamental shapes in geometry, made of just three straight sides connected at three corners. The corners, or vertices, are single points, while the sides connecting them are one-dimensional line segments. If you add up the three inside angles of any triangle, they will always equal a straight line—180 degrees. As a flat, two-dimensional shape, its interior forms a planar region. You can pick any side to call the base, making the opposite point the apex, and the shortest distance between the two is the height. Finding the area is easy, too—it's just half of the base length multiplied by the height.

Fun Facts About Triangles

Here's some stuff about triangles that you probabloy didn't know:

  • The Strongest Shape: Triangles are the only rigid polygons that cannot be bent or deformed without changing the lengths of their sides. If you pin three sticks together, the shape locks firmly in place, which is why bridges, cranes, and roof trusses use them for strength.
  • Euler’s Line: In any non-equilateral triangle, three important centers—the centroid (center of mass), the circumcenter (center of the circumscribed circle), and the orthocenter (where the altitudes meet)—all sit in a straight line known as Euler’s Line.
  • The Triangle Inequality: You cannot make a triangle out of just any three lengths. The sum of the lengths of any two sides must always be longer than the third.
